# Finance, Accounting, and Analytics Requirements

## Financial management

Financial overview includes total revenue, expenses, net income, gross profit,
operating expenses, net profit, cash and bank balances, receivables, and
payables. Calculations use consistent accounting rules.

### Income

Record sales, other, service, interest, and miscellaneous income with date,
category, amount, payment account, reference, description, attachment, and
responsible user. POS revenue must not be counted twice.

### Expenses

Support categories for rent, salaries, utilities, transportation, marketing,
maintenance, office supplies, equipment, taxes, and other expenses. Provide
creation, approval, recurrence, attachments, payment status, branch allocation,
and expense reports.

### Cash, bank, receivables, payables, and budgets

- Multiple cash and bank accounts, deposits, withdrawals, transfers,
  reconciliation, and transaction history
- Customer invoices, due dates, partial payments, balances, overdue invoices,
  reminders, and customer statements
- Supplier invoices, due dates, partial payments, balances, payments, and
  supplier statements
- Annual, monthly, branch, and expense-category budgets with actual-versus-budget
  comparison and variance

## Double-entry accounting

Use a chart of accounts with Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Revenue, and Expenses.
Authorized accountants can configure account codes and categories.

The general ledger records journal entry number, transaction date, account,
debit, credit, description, reference, branch, source module, and creator.
Support manual and automated journals, adjusting and reversing entries, and
opening balances. Every journal entry must balance:

`Total Debits = Total Credits`

Posted entries cannot be silently edited or deleted. Correct them through
reversal or adjustment entries. Control period closing, retain source references
for automated postings, and reconcile reports to ledger balances.

## Financial statements

- Profit and loss: revenue, returns, net revenue, cost of goods sold, gross
  profit, operating expenses, other income/expenses, and net profit
- Balance sheet: current/non-current assets, current/non-current liabilities,
  and equity
- Cash flow: operating, investing, and financing activities, opening and closing
  balances
- Trial balance, general ledger, account ledger, journal report, and AR/AP aging

Calculate COGS, gross profit, and net profit only when valid inventory costs and
source data exist. Distinguish recorded from calculated values.

## Analytics

### Sales

Revenue by day, month, and year; by category, product, branch, and cashier;
payment-method distribution; returns; and discounts.

### Products and inventory

Best sellers, slow movers, low-margin items, revenue, quantity sold, and
profitability when valid costs exist. Inventory analytics include valuation,
turnover, aging, low-stock analysis, movement trends, dead stock, and reorder
recommendations.

### Finance and branches

Revenue versus expenses, profit and cash-flow trends, budget variance, AR/AP
aging, expense categories, and branch comparisons for sales, revenue, expenses,
profit, inventory, cash flow, and employee performance. Support date and
organizational filters.
